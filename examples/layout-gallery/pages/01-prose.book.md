A status report summarizes the state of a project at a point in time. It
exists to give stakeholders a shared, accurate picture of progress,
risks, and upcoming work without requiring them to inspect internal
tools directly.

## Purpose

Reports answer three questions: what was planned, what actually
happened, and what happens next. A good report is short, factual, and
consistent in structure so readers can compare one period against the
last.

## Recommended structure

- **Summary**: two to four sentences covering the headline state.
- **Progress**: completed items with dates, not effort claims.
- **Risks**: concrete risks with owners and mitigation steps.
- **Next period**: committed items only, not aspirations.

Keep the cadence fixed and the format identical between periods. Readers
build intuition for where to look when the layout never changes.
